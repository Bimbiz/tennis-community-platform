## Interview 3

**Interviewer:** How do you currently find people to play tennis with or organize matches for others?

**Respondent:** Basically, when I was playing, I was playing with my coach, and I didn't really think about organizing matches, because I'm just a player. I've played in some tournaments, but I think that's it.

**Interviewer:** What, if anything, makes it difficult to find suitable opponents or playing partners? Can you describe a recent example?

**Respondent:** I think it can be hard to find suitable opponents or partners because of a possible skill gap. For example, if you just talk to people to find out what level they are, some people will say they're bad, and they're actually good. And the exact opposite: some people will brag about how good they are, and they'll be bad.

**Interviewer:** How do you currently assess your own or another player's skill level? What information do you rely on?

**Respondent:** Usually I'd get that information from my coach. But if we're talking about my own decisions, then I'd probably just ask them, ask their Elo, and so on.

**Interviewer:** Have you used any player rating or level system? If so, how did it affect your choice of matches or tournaments? If not, how do you decide whether players are at similar levels?

**Respondent:** The last time I played in a tournament, it was a King of the Court format, so there was a level system, but I didn't have a rating. It kind of affected my choice of matches and tournaments, because you usually don't get to play in very big tournaments if you're an amateur.

**Interviewer:** Have you participated in or organized amateur tennis tournaments? If yes, how did registration, scheduling and communication work? If not, what has influenced your decision not to participate?

**Respondent:** I've played in amateur tennis tournaments. Registration was easy, and scheduling wasn't really hard either. The hardest part was probably getting to talk to the coach so he could organize it, because the whole place where we played had to organize the event.

**Interviewer:** Based on your experience, what parts of joining or running a tournament are most inconvenient, if any?

**Respondent:** In my opinion, the most inconvenient thing about joining is getting into teams. If you're playing doubles, you have to find a person who's at your skill level, and so on.

**Interviewer:** How do you usually find a doubles partner, if you play doubles? And what would you consider important when choosing or being assigned one?

**Respondent:** Usually, if I'm playing doubles, I try to look for people I've already played with. And if I haven't, I'll just choose whoever played better when I was watching them.

**Interviewer:** If you used a mobile app for amateur tennis activities, which tasks would you want it to help with, and why?

**Respondent:** In my opinion, a tennis app should be able to put people into brackets, find them an opponent and maybe a partner, and also have an Elo system. That's basically it.

**Interviewer:** Which of the needs we discussed matter most to you? What concerns, if any, would affect your willingness to use such an app?

**Respondent:** I think what matters most to me is the partner or opponent search, because it's hard to find a good opponent or a good teammate to play with. Thank you very much.
